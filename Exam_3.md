### final review

- energy vs nutrient dense
- types of studies
    - lab studies
        - test tube vs animal
    - human studies
        - observational (report correlation, not causation) 
            - case-control studies (ppl with conditions are compared to ppl without to find the differences)
            - cohort (large group of ppl over time. retrospective or prospective)
            - positive correlation = A and B increase/decrease in the *same direction*
            - negative correlation = A and B change in *opposite directions*
        - experimental
- food groups
    - MyPlate (fruit, grain, veg, protein, dairy)
- phytochemicals: substances in plants that aren't nutrients, but could be good for you (caffine, beta carotene, organosulfides, etc)

malnutrition and overnutrition

EAR = estimated average requirement (meets needs of 50% of pop)
RDA = recommended daily ave (meet most ppls recs without going over)
AI = average intake, not enough data for rda but this amount seems safe
UL = upper limit, going over this can lead to toxicity



carbs
- simple (mono- and di-saccharides)
- complex 
    - polysaccharides (starch, glycogen, fiber)
    - oligosaccharides (3-10 monosaccharides, raffinose, stachyose)

insulin = lower blood glucose (after eating), gets glucose into cells, promotes glycogenesis/protein synthesis/lipid storage
glucagon = raise blood glucose (haven't eaten in a while), glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis (break down protein/fat, may lead to ketone formation)

diabetes
- type 1 (autoimmune disease, insufficient insulin, needs insulin injection or pump)  
- type 2 (risk factors: physical inactivity/overweight/family history, insulin resistant, treatment of diet/exercise/meds)
